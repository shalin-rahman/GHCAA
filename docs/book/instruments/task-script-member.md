# Task script: ordinary member

Six tasks, about twenty-five minutes. Run at least half of these sessions on a phone.

Read each task aloud and also hand it to the participant in writing, so they can re-read it without
asking you. Give one task at a time. Do not say which menu, page or button to use: the wording below
is deliberately in the member's language rather than the platform's, because finding the right place
is part of what is being measured.

If a task depends on the one before it and the participant failed that one, set the state up
yourself, say that you are doing so, and mark the next task as started from a repaired state.

---

## Task 1. Join

**Start state:** signed out, on the public home page.

> You have heard about the alumni platform from a friend and you want to join. Create an account for
> yourself and get as far as you can.

**Success:** account created, one-time code received and entered, and the participant arrives at
their portal.

**Watch for:** whether they find registration at all from the home page; whether the one-time code
arrives before they give up waiting; whether they understand that a membership tier is assigned by
the Association and is not theirs to choose. That last point is DC-06 and is the most common source
of confusion this task is designed to expose.

**Record:** time, outcome, where they went first, what they said about the code.

---

## Task 2. Say who you are

**Start state:** signed in, profile largely empty.

> Fill in enough about yourself that other alumni could recognise you and get in touch. Add a photo
> if you would normally.

**Success:** profile saved with name, batch, contact and at least one further field, and the
participant believes it is saved.

**Watch for:** whether they can tell what is required and what is optional; whether the photo upload
works on their device; whether they trust that it saved. Note anything they refuse to enter and why,
since that is data-minimisation evidence for §8.11 and is worth more than the completion time.

---

## Task 3. Pay the subscription

**Start state:** signed in, at least one due showing.

> You want to pay your membership subscription. You have already sent the money from your mobile
> wallet. Tell the Association you have paid, so the Treasurer can confirm it.

**Success:** a payment declaration recorded against the correct due, with the transaction reference
and a proof image attached.

**Watch for:** whether they expect to pay inside the platform and are confused when they cannot. The
platform holds no gateway credentials, so payment happens outside it and is declared afterwards.
Whether that reads as a limitation or as a fraud risk to the member is the single most important
thing this study can tell us about the manual-verification design, and it goes to §8.9 and §12.10.
Record their words about it as closely as you can.

---

## Task 4. Come to the reunion

**Start state:** signed in, at least one upcoming event open for registration.

> There is a reunion coming up. Find out when and where it is, and put your name down for it.

**Success:** registered for the correct event, and able to say when and where it is without being
prompted.

**Watch for:** whether the date and venue are found before the registration button; whether a
waitlist, if they hit one, is understood as a waitlist.

---

## Task 5. Find a classmate

**Start state:** signed in.

> Find someone from your own batch in the member directory and see how you would contact them.

**Success:** the right person located and their contact route found.

**Watch for:** whether the search is used or the list scrolled; whether they expect to see contact
details that the platform does not show. What they expect to see about another member is privacy
evidence and should be written down verbatim.

---

## Task 6. Check the rules

**Start state:** signed out, on the public site, so this task also tests the public view.

> Someone told you the Association's rules about voting changed. Find the current rules and see
> whether you are allowed to vote.

**Success:** the constitution reached, and the participant can say which version is in force and
whether their tier can vote.

**Watch for:** whether they can tell the current version from a superseded one. DC-16 says the
reader always resolves the version in force, and this task is the only direct check of whether that
design decision works for a reader rather than for the database.

---

## Closing

Then the SUS form, then the final open question in the briefing script. Do not discuss the tasks
before the SUS form is complete.
