# Consent and briefing

Read this aloud. Do not paraphrase it, and do not shorten it for people you know well, which will be
most of them.

## Briefing script

> I am testing the alumni platform, not you. If something is hard to use, that is the platform's
> fault and it is exactly what I need to find out. There is no way for you to get this wrong.
>
> I will give you a few tasks, one at a time. Please try to do them as you would at home, and say
> out loud what you are thinking as you go, including when you are confused or annoyed. If you cannot
> work out how to do something, tell me and we will move on. That is a useful result, not a failure
> on your part.
>
> I will not be able to help you during the tasks. That is not me being difficult. If I help, I
> learn nothing about whether the platform works without me sitting next to you, which is the whole
> question.
>
> I will time each task and take notes. I am not recording audio or video. Afterwards there is a
> short ten-question form, and a few questions about your work in the Association.
>
> Nothing you say will be attributed to you by name. The Association is small, so I will also avoid
> writing down anything that would identify you by your role where the role is not the point.
>
> You can stop at any time, skip any task or any question, and ask me to discard what we have done so
> far. You do not have to give a reason. It will not affect anything else.
>
> Do you agree to take part on that basis?

Record the answer as yes or no in the response log. Consent is verbal, as §4.9 states. Do not collect
a signature: a signed form in a small association creates a named record of participation, which is
more personal data than the study needs.

## If the participant asks whether the author will see their answers

Answer honestly: yes, because the author is the only person running the study. Say that responses are
reported without names and in aggregate, and that they are free to decline. Do not claim an
independence the study does not have.

## After the session

> Thank you. Is there anything the platform does badly that I did not ask you about?

Write the answer down verbatim where you can. What people say at the end, without being
asked, is usually the most useful thing in the whole session, and it is the first thing lost if you
write the note up later from memory.
