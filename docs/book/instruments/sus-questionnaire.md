# System Usability Scale

Brooke's ten-item scale, unchanged in wording except that "system" is replaced by "the platform".
The items alternate between positive and negative on purpose. Do not reorder them, do not drop the
negative ones, and do not reword an item to be clearer: the scoring rule and the published benchmark
of 68 both depend on the instrument staying as published.

Give this to the participant immediately after their last task, before any discussion. Discussion
first moves the answers.

## Instructions to the participant

Mark one box per line, based on how you felt while using the platform just now. Answer quickly rather
than carefully. If you are unsure about a line, mark the middle box and move on. Do not leave a line
blank.

| | Statement | Strongly disagree 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | Strongly agree 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | I think that I would like to use the platform frequently. | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 2 | I found the platform unnecessarily complex. | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 3 | I thought the platform was easy to use. | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 4 | I think that I would need the support of a technical person to be able to use the platform. | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |

## Scoring

For the odd-numbered items, subtract one from the mark. For the even-numbered items, subtract the
mark from five. Add the ten adjusted values, which gives a number between zero and forty, and
multiply by two and a half. The result is between zero and one hundred.

It is not a percentage and must never be written as one. A score of 68 is the published average
across a large body of studies, which is what NFR-U3 compares against.

Report the mean across participants with the number of participants beside it, and report the
individual scores as well. With fewer than about a dozen responses the mean can move several points
on one answer, and showing the spread is the only honest way to present it.

## Bangla wording

Where a participant is more comfortable in Bangla, read the item aloud in Bangla and mark the box for
them, keeping the meaning as close to the English as the sentence allows. Note on the form which
items were read rather than read by the participant, since a read item and a self-completed item are
not quite the same measurement.
